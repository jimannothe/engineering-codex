## Resume Improvement Rules

- Lead with the role title and the most relevant outcomes.
- Prefer active verbs and concrete actions.
- Keep bullets short and specific.
- Keep the top of the resume aligned to the target lane.
- Do not add tools or domain claims that are not real.
- If a bullet is broad, rewrite it around the job's language.

## STAR Rules

- Situation: give the minimum context needed.
- Task: state the problem or responsibility.
- Action: explain exactly what you did.
- Result: explain what changed because of your work.
- Keep the answer honest and measurable where possible.
- End with the outcome, not with a vague lesson.

## STAR Story Bank

### 1. Turning validation work into repeatable automation

- **Situation:** In validation work, testing and data collection involved repeated manual steps across hardware, firmware, operating systems, networking, and I/O.
- **Task:** Make the workflow more repeatable and reduce the manual overhead of test execution and data collection.
- **Action:** Built Python automation tools to streamline test execution and data collection, then used logs and telemetry to support investigation and verification.
- **Result:** The validation workflow became more efficient and consistent, and the team had a more repeatable way to run and analyze tests.

### 2. Isolating complex platform failures

- **Situation:** Platform failures appeared across server-validation work and required deeper analysis than a simple pass/fail check.
- **Task:** Identify the root cause and help the team converge on a fix.
- **Action:** Analyzed logs, telemetry, and hardware diagnostics, reproduced the issue where possible, and worked with engineering teams to document and validate the findings.
- **Result:** The team could move from symptoms to a more actionable diagnosis and validate fixes with less guesswork.

### 3. Restoring productivity in a support environment

- **Situation:** In the service desk role, users were blocked by Windows, Active Directory, hardware, software, and network issues.
- **Task:** Triage incidents quickly and restore productivity while meeting service-level expectations.
- **Action:** Diagnosed the issue, followed the incident path, communicated clearly with users, and documented the resolution for future reference.
- **Result:** Issues were resolved in an organized way and users could return to work faster.

### 4. Catching design issues before manufacturing

- **Situation:** As an applications design engineer, OEM and ODM partners were developing Intel-based PC and server platforms where design mistakes could surface late.
- **Task:** Review schematics and layouts early enough to identify risks before they turned into manufacturing or integration problems.
- **Action:** Reviewed hardware schematics and PCB layouts in OrCAD and debugged hardware, BIOS/firmware, and software issues during platform integration and customer bring-up.
- **Result:** Design issues could be identified earlier, reducing downstream integration risk and improving bring-up quality.

### 5. Validating and documenting platform defects

- **Situation:** During Intel validation work, platform defects had to be reproduced and explained clearly enough for engineering teams to act on them.
- **Task:** Help the team understand the defect, reproduce it, and move toward a fix.
- **Action:** Worked with engineering teams to reproduce and document platform defects, using platform logs and diagnostics to support the analysis.
- **Result:** The defect investigation became easier to follow and the team had stronger evidence for resolution.

### 6. Working across cross-functional teams

- **Situation:** Validation and support problems often crossed multiple teams and disciplines.
- **Task:** Keep collaboration moving so fixes could be validated and documented without losing context.
- **Action:** Coordinated with cross-functional teams to validate fixes, share findings, and keep the investigation organized.
- **Result:** Communication stayed clear and the team could close the loop on issues more reliably.
